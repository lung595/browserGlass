# Roadmap

These are ideas, not promises, and they have no dates. Anything that would need the network while the plugin runs, or would send data somewhere, is out of scope.

## Planned

- ~~**Signed companion add-on**~~ (done in 1.4.2): Mozilla signs the companion, shipped as `companion/browserglass@companion.xpi`, so *Live control* works on Firefox release, ESR and Beta builds, LibreWolf shows it as verified, and `xpinstall.signatures.required` is no longer written.
- **New screenshots**: a GIF of the launcher, and the *Sites* settings with the new page layers.
- **Live control in Flatpak and Snap browsers**: through the WebExtensions portal or a path shared with the sandbox, since the helper cannot run inside it today.
- **Live control checked on Zen, Floorp and Waterfox**: it is built for every Firefox-family browser but tested on Firefox and LibreWolf only.
- **Glass in frames**: since 1.3.1 every frame keeps its own background (the add-on marks it excluded), so its text stays readable; giving frames the glass would need their own tone and layers.
- **Snap Firefox in the niri rule**: add the Snap app-id (`firefox_firefox`) once it is confirmed on a real Snap install. Until then, see *Known limits*.
- **Live checks on more installs**: Zen, Floorp and Waterfox, plus the Flatpak and Snap variants. Their profile folders come from the browsers' documentation and are tested on a fake home only.
- **Dark Reader and local addresses**: its stylesheet fetch runs with the add-on's access, so a page that links a stylesheet on the local network or on `localhost` gets its text copied in. Refuse loopback and private targets (and redirects to them).
- **Waterfox's own defaults for the add-on signature guard**: the guard assumes Firefox's defaults for every family; use per-family defaults once Waterfox's are confirmed in its source.
- **Daemon triggers in CONTRIBUTING**: describe the daemon's real triggers and its 10 s poll.
- **Lighter companion script**: group the tone checks that follow changes on `<html>`; load Dark Reader only in the pages it darkens (the layer pass a `scroll` asks for already runs once per 300 ms at most).
- **A steady LibreWolf end-to-end run**: `test_own_surfaces` now and then reads the page's background halfway through its fade.
- **Light mode in the end-to-end test**: reader view and PDFs are checked in dark mode only (`tests/e2e/`).
- **Hide footers, as an option**: about 250 of the bundled my-internet styles have a *no footer* block (`footer { display: none }`, Heise's among them). The sync keeps only their transparency features, so those blocks are never applied and footers, with their legal and consent-revoke links, stay. A setting, off by default, could apply them for those who want the shorter pages.
- **Choose the profiles**: an option to leave some profiles of a browser without glass.
- ~~**Site theme presets**~~ (done in 1.4): under a dark theme the add-on writes a site's own stored theme value (a generic rule for common storage keys, recipes per site in `data/themes.json`, cookies included) and puts it back afterwards. Still out of reach: a signed-in account whose theme comes from the server (a recipe skips the write), cookies the site sets `HttpOnly`, and a stored choice of the default that cannot be told from no choice at all. See *Sites with their own theme menu* in the [user guide](docs/GUIDE.md#light-pages-under-a-dark-theme).
- **The DMS plugin registry**: submit the plugin once it has had wider use.

## Not planned

- **Chromium-based browsers**: they have no `userChrome.css` or `userContent.css` to paint the window and the pages with.
- **Restarting the browser automatically**: that would close your windows and tabs. The badge and the toast say when a restart is needed, and you choose when to restart.
- **Downloading styles at run time**: the my-internet styles are bundled in `data/` and refreshed through a pull request (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#releasing-a-version)).
- **Compositors other than niri**: the glass depends on a niri window rule.

## Known limits

- **A manual restart is needed.** Firefox reads its CSS only when it starts. After a change (a new wallpaper, the opacity, a setting), a running browser shows the new look only after a restart. When the glass turns on, niri applies the plugin's rule at once, so a running browser stays opaque until its restart. The exceptions: the light or dark switch (with DMS's *Sync with portal* on), and site exclusions with Live control.
- **`gs on` and `gs off` are not live**: the browser's own bars are read at start-up, so they follow at the next restart; the niri rule changes at once.
- **Live control from a changed `extension/` needs an add-on that loads unsigned.** The signed `companion/browserglass@companion.xpi` (since 1.4.2) is used only while it matches `extension/`. Without a match (1.3.0 to 1.4.1 ship none, and a local change to `extension/` breaks it), Browser Glass places the unsigned companion and sets `xpinstall.signatures.required` to `false`, as in 1.2: Firefox release, ESR and Beta builds refuse it, so there the launcher's changes apply at the next restart, and LibreWolf shows it as *could not be verified*.
- **Private windows have no live control**: the companion does not run there and their site cannot be read, so `gs blist` refuses in a private window, and a private window shows exclusion changes after a restart.
- **Frames stay opaque**: with Live control, frames inside a page (a captcha, an embedded video or form) keep their own background; without it they follow the generic rule.
- **Bars laid in the flow**: a web app's sidebar or header that is neither fixed nor sticky keeps its own colours (no uniform veil), since it cannot be told from a panel of the page.
- **A modal dialog over a page whose body paints no colour**: while a modal dialog is open, the page under it drops out of hit tests, so text over a picture cannot be told from text on the page. The body's opaque colour then gives the tone. A page whose colour comes from a wrapper instead falls back to its text, and white headlines over pictures can tip it dark until the dialog closes.
- **Not every piece of site chrome gets the bar's veil yet**: a fixed or sticky bar along an edge gets the 65% veil and blur, and so do the floating layers found on top of the page (menus, mega menus, popovers, drawers, dialogs, cookie banners, search flyouts and suggestion lists), and so does the site's header at the top of the page, in the flow or before it sticks, with a header in the flow right under it holding the site's menus (Python's tall blue header under its strip of links), a row of three or more links at the top with no `nav` (Arch Linux's `#archnavbar`), the header under a cookie wall covering the window (Ars Technica's), and a band in the flow right under a veiled bar within the top third of the page (GitHub's repository header, Swissinfo's menu row). The page's first header holding the site's menus gets it too under an empty or ad slot at the top, within half the window (NZZ's), and the page's bars keep it under a modal dialog or a page root made inert (Booking's sheet), found by their style then, in the first 4000 elements of the body at most. A header in the flow lower than that, taller than the window, or in the page's main content keeps the site's own fill, and one in the flow under a sticky header loses the veil once scrolled under it. The menus behind a consent wall in a cross-origin frame (the Guardian's main menu and edition picker) are unverified: a check cannot open them without consenting. A floating layer is found also inside a zero-size positioner, as the panel in the flow of a see-through positioner larger than it (Galaxus' popovers, the DSFR mega menus), within a veiled header over the header's own text (python.org's menus over its hero), as a dialog in a window-wide backdrop of no z-index (GitHub's), and when a button with `aria-expanded=true` or a checked `aria-haspopup` box beside it shows it (Arch Linux's language list). The veil goes on the panel that paints it, inside a see-through wrapper or column too and in an open shadow root (GitLab's search box, SBB's drawer, Reddit's user menu); a see-through wrapper holding two painted parts side by side (a header and a body apart) gets a veil on each part, the gap between them left bare. It is missed when it is smaller than 96 px square (a short dropdown of a few links) and is no toast or tooltip in a plain opaque colour at a `z-index` of 20 or more among the body's first two levels, when it is laid absolutely without a menu or dialog role, nothing marks it shown, it does not hang out of the site's header or nav, is no popover placed from the body at a `z-index` of 100 or more and no painted side panel half the window's height along its edge, when its content sits in a cross-origin frame (the Guardian's Sourcepoint consent) or a closed shadow root, and a fixed drawer shaped like a sidebar (as tall as the window along its edge) is judged as a bar: veiled when it holds the site's menus (a `nav` or `role=navigation`) or covers content, so a see-through one with neither stays bare glass, and an empty see-through one (nothing shown in it: a site's positioner left fixed for its popovers, empty or holding a closed one) is no bar. A search flyout hanging out of a bar and laid from the bar's top edge (Apple's) doubles the veil over the bar's height, and a page's dimming curtain behind a search or a drawer (Apple's black at 0.4, Digitec's black scrim behind its header, Amazon's at 0.8 behind its menu, NZZ's) keeps its own colour as a scrim: no norm sets the scrim's colour. A search popup that is a modal `<dialog>` in the top layer (Decathlon's, MDN's in a shadow root) gets the veil's computed style, but its blur was only judged by computed style, since headless snapshots do not paint `backdrop-filter`, and the same snapshots paint page cards over Tailwind's search modal where hit tests find the modal on top. The blur is painted by the bar's `::before` (so the bar is neither the Backdrop Root of a menu or drawer in it nor the containing block of its fixed parts, such as frame.work's scrim), which replaces a `::before` the site draws on the bar itself (a rule or an icon there is lost; its own size no longer shrinks the blur), and a static bar becomes `relative`. A static bar holding an absolute part the page places from outside it (Apple's nav on its 404 page) is left static instead, with the veil's colour but no blur. A header in the flow lifted over the page takes the highest `z-index` of what it holds (1 at least), so it paints over page content laid lower than its own menus, and under page content laid higher than all of them. A menu closed by its content's opacity or visibility loses the veil; one closed only by moving it off-screen or shrinking it under 96 px square does too, but one its site leaves shown and empty keeps it. A hover menu is looked for again half a second after the last hover, so one opening later than that, with no other event, is missed. Left as found in a live sweep: YouTube's player settings menu (part of the video's own controls), Notion's cookie panel (out of reach in a frame or closed shadow root), Arch Linux's sticky panel shorter than the window (neither a bar nor a sidebar), Airbnb's search pill (a control, not chrome), and Etsy (behind a captcha), unverified. Swissinfo's fixed bottom banner (`div.temp-sticky-banner`, opaque `rgb(246, 246, 249)`, full width) is not veiled in either theme, and it catches the clicks meant for the Menu button. frame.work and rust-lang.org read as light pages under a light theme; MediaMarkt in its Black Friday dress (a near-black section over most of its white page) reads as a dark page and gets the dark veil, its own dark style (a tone limit: a page mostly painted dark in sections is dark). A sidebar more than 24 px off the window's edge (inside a page column centred in a wide window) or in the flow is no bar. The tucked row is found by hit tests across the bar, so it is only cut where it is hit under the bar's own elements: Ricardo's category row is cut in a wide real window but was left whole in a 1400 by 900 headless check, where those hit tests found other elements first. The veil's tone is that of the page: a page whose own colours change late through a class on an element deeper than `<body>` or a style sheet a script adopts (not one in `<head>`) keeps the tone first measured, and its bars that veil, until the next measure (a click, a navigation, a resize). Still open after the last live sweep: MediaMarkt's menu drawer is solid white and its promo strip in the header keeps its fill; 20 Minuten's sticky header is veiled only once stuck; LEGO's age-gate dialog keeps its two brand-colour halves and its black cookie row; eBay's category row under the header and a header narrower than the window (Tages-Anzeiger's centred 1100 px box) read as a second tone.
- **Text is judged against the glass over a black or a white wallpaper**: the page cannot read the wallpaper, so the add-on judges text against the glass over the worst one (the light tint over black, the dark one over white), and the light glass over a light wallpaper gets greys a little darker than it needs. A bar's light veil is judged over black, the worst of what can scroll under it (a red thumbnail, a site's dark code window, a playing video, a site's scrim, the glass over a dark wallpaper), so its greys and links are a little darker than the veil over a light page needs; the dark veil is judged over that glass, without the blur's own flood of the glass colour (as a capture without the backdrop filter shows it). A see-through backing (a box in black at 5 %, a white card at 60 %) is judged as it shows over the glass over the worst wallpaper. Text a site's own scrim covers (Decathlon's nav labels and promo strip under its mega menu's and search's dimming layers) is left as the scrim dims it. A site's own selected row and band keep their colours: Tailwind's selected DocSearch hit (white on sky blue, 2.7:1) and python.org's light links on its blue header seen through the glass (about 4.3:1). A private window with an opaque tint (Private glass off) is judged as if it were see-through. On Ricardo's light page the category row and the listings' meta lines (#7a7a7a, #717171) were not lifted in a live check, which the fixture does not reproduce.
- **Some effective colours are not modelled**: text is judged at its own colour and the opacity of its wrappers up to the first opaque box, the first colour of a gradient clipped to it, its `::placeholder` and a focused element's outline. A `mix-blend-mode` on text, a site's own `::selection` colours and white text on a gradient backing of its own (a violet call to action) are left as the site draws them. A wrapper fainter than 40 % is taken as a fade in progress and not counted. When no shade makes a faded text read, only the opacity of the text's own element is raised: a fade set on a wrapper of several lines stays.
- **A bar painted by a child of its own colour**: a fixed or sticky bar whose background is drawn by a child that covers it gets the veil, but that child goes see-through only when it paints the page's own colour. A child in another colour (a brand-coloured band inside a see-through header) keeps painting over the veil, so the bar keeps its colour, without the blur (DER SPIEGEL's compact orange header, its sticky header see-through round a `#header-bar` painted in the brand's orange).
- **Light glyphs on the light glass**: a bar the site paints dark gets the light veil under a light theme; its white artwork of one tone now follows its links' dark ink, but white artwork in many tones, an icon whitened by a filter other than an invert or a brightness, and a CSS background picture larger than an icon still read poorly on it. The white artwork follows a bar, or a header in the flow the glass cleared, whose links got the dark colour, and a picture straight on the light glass (no fill of the site's under it) whatever its labels; one on a box of the site's own fill is left as drawn; icon-font glyphs (an empty `<i>` or `<span>` drawing its `::before` or `::after`) get the ink as text does, but one drawn in a colour other than its element's is not read. A picture whose light grey part, half its ink at least, is lost beside colour parts gets a dark backing (python.org's word mark), and so does an inline svg whose lost light grey shapes, not laid within another of its shapes, cover 40 % of the boxes of its shapes (Ars Technica's white "TECHNICA" beside its orange disc); a colour logo whose white is a smaller part (PyPI's) is left as drawn. A white logo drawn in many tones reads poorly on the light glass alike (the white half of RTS's PLAY RTS wordmark on its light page). Toggles drawn for a dark band (YouTube's pale grey off switches, a white knob on a light track in the player's settings) and the pale `>` chevrons its settings rows paint as a background beside their value are left as drawn, as are the dark time and chapter pills of the player's controls under its light settings veil. What a site hides under an opaque drawer shows faintly through the drawer's 65 % veil (frame.work's header word mark under its mobile drawer reads mid grey): it is the veil's see-through, not an ink to fix. A chart's coloured bars are a colour graphic, left as drawn (`#3b82f6` reads 2.18:1 on the light glass over a dark wallpaper). A chevron drawn through a CSS mask is read only on a button or link's `::before` and `::after` painted in the element's colour. A drop-down field (`<select>`) is inked as text is, its open list then shown in the system's colours rather than the site's.
- **Light pages' own white panels stay solid under a light theme**: on a page Dark Reader darkens, a white block gets a light veil, but under a light theme (and on a site's own dark theme) the page's own cards and panels are left as the site paints them (Raiffeisen's full-width bank selector stays a solid white panel on the light glass, YouTube's lone sign-in nudge a dark one). Only a row of two or more cards in the page's plain colour (SRF's teasers, Docker's tutorial cards, crates.io's panels), a slight tint allowed (PyPI's sidebar cards), or a lone module across most of the window and lower than it (Heise's modules) gets the veil, and under a light theme a light tinted panel alone right on the glass, 40 px high at least (GitHub's grey header of a file list, service-public.fr's "France services" panel), under a dark tone a dark tinted strip alone there, 40 to 64 px high (GitHub's dark latest-commit box), and a white field or light secondary button there (judged again once its own fill turns brand or dark, so a button filled only after hydration keeps its colour; one whose fill changes without any pass running after keeps the veil until the next pass); a lone narrower panel on a box of the site's own, a section's card taller than the window, or one in another colour would also change the boxes of GitHub, YouTube, LDLC or admin.ch, which read well as they are, so it is not done generically. So a lone card in a row of photo tiles keeps its fill (IKEA's "Toutes les idées de cadeaux" card, `rgb(36, 36, 36)` in its dark theme, among picture links), and so does a light card laid on a site's own dark page (IKEA's `rgb(238, 238, 238)` panel), one colour among others not the page's tone. A card drawn by a box round its link and outlined by a border of its own keeps its fill too, since GitHub's pinned repositories are such boxes in the page's colour, which cannot always be read under the site's own style.
- **Buttons on a white card left by a site's own dark version keep their fill**: under a light theme a light secondary button or field on a framed light card the page keeps solid, the card itself on the glass (in the page or an open shadow tree), is veiled with the card showing through it, and so is a search pill painted by its own box or by empty layers under it. Under a site's own dark tone, a white card its dark version leaves (a section drawn late, its own class not switched) keeps its light buttons as solid slabs, since a dark veil there would mix the two tones on one card; a button on a card that is not framed, or on one laid on another box of the site's own, keeps its fill too.
- **Darkened blocks without text stay solid**: on a page Dark Reader darkens, a card gets its light veil only when it holds text near the window; an empty block, one holding only pictures, and a card inside a floating element (an absolutely placed layer with a z-index, a fixed or sticky one) keep Dark Reader's opaque dark panel.
- **Brand buttons drawn late keep Dark Reader's colours**: a small button in a saturated colour keeps its fill and its label's colour only when it is on the page when Dark Reader starts, since its colours are read just before. One drawn later (PostFinance's cookie banner, a view of a web app opened afterwards) gets Dark Reader's duller colours: reading the page's own colours back would mean switching Dark Reader's styles off and restyling the whole page, transitions held, for each new button, too costly on a page that keeps adding links. A kept button also keeps its resting colour on hover. A button drawn as a styled label inside a large link (PostFinance's hero teaser, a whole card made a link) is not a control of button size, so it is not kept either. The same holds for text over a picture of the page's own: only the text on the page when Dark Reader starts keeps its colour, and only when the picture is a background image of an element at most 1.5 windows tall (text over an `<img>` laid under it is read as text on the page). A focus ring under Dark Reader always gets the light colour, also around a light button it would not need it on, and not inside a shadow tree.
- **Buttons whose fill is not their own background**: a dark button keeps its colours with a thin light ring, and a pastel one its colours, only when the button (or a `::before` or `::after` covering it) paints its fill. A button filled by a layer of a component library (Migros' Angular Material "Voir 21 produits", see-through in both themes, its grey drawn by an inner state layer) is not seen, and Dark Reader darkens its grey to the card's colour, so it reads as text. A button made see-through again (`clear`) also loses a hover fill Dark Reader would have darkened.
- **A label on a page column keeps a veil under Dark Reader**: a small label painted in the colour of a white page column (ch.ch's tagline) goes see-through with the column under a light theme, but on a page Dark Reader darkens it is veiled as a block first and shows as a dark rectangle on the glass.
- **Text over pictures**: the contrast check leaves text over a background picture alone, and Dark Reader can make such text hard to read (Amazon's banners).
- **Blended photos not always tiled on busy shop pages**: on Amazon, darkened, the number of product photos blended with `multiply` that get their white tile varies from one load to the next (some complete photos on a card left untiled while those of the card beside it are); they then show their white wiped out on the dark glass. Not yet traced.
- **Product photos touching their edges**: on a page Dark Reader darkens, a product photo shot on white gets its white tile only when its outer edge is mostly white (80 %). One whose product fills the frame to its edges (a bag of nuts cropped tight on Migros) stays a plain white rectangle on the dark card: telling it from an editorial photo would need more than its edge.
- **Black pictures from another site**: a black logo or line drawing on a transparent background is inverted or tiled only when its pixels can be read: a picture of the page's own site, one embedded in the page, or one whose server lets any site read it (CORS). The add-on's site access (`*://*/*`) does not let it read any other picture served from another site (a CDN): Firefox lifts that only for `<all_urls>`, a permission change that would prompt on update. Such a picture and a CSS background picture stay as they are and can read poorly on a dark page; so does an inline SVG drawing in dark colours of its own outside a button or a link.
- **Sign-in prompts in frames** (Google's "Sign in with Google" box, for example) keep their own white background on a darkened page, as other frames do.
- **Shadow trees follow at the next restart**: a live exclusion leaves the parts a site draws in its shadow DOM styled until the browser restarts, since they do not see the page's `data-browserglass-off` mark.
- **Shadow trees cannot tell a light page from a dark one**: the parts a site draws in its shadow DOM do not see the page's dark-version markers or the companion's tone mark (only the bars' colour reaches them, as a custom property). Under a dark theme the generic style therefore leaves them alone on every page (as in 1.0), and a dedicated site style still applies there on every page, light-only ones included.
- **Pages built in shadow DOM (web components) are not darkened**: the companion glasses the open shadow roots of a page (page layers, bars, fills, ink, icons), but they stay out of reach of the page stylesheet and of Dark Reader, so under a dark theme such a light page keeps its own light cards. The Home Assistant demo is an example: it starts in its own dark theme only because a recipe writes its stored theme, and a Home Assistant server of your own, whose theme comes from your account, keeps its light cards. Its toolbar, see-through over the page's layer, gets no veil.
- **Low panels keep their fill on a page left see-through**: under a light theme or a site's own dark theme, a panel round text gets the light veil only when it is at least 64 px high and 120 px wide, and is one of two such panels of its colour near the window or a module across most of it, so a lower panel keeps the site's colour, except a light one alone right on the glass under a light theme, veiled from 40 px high (a card drawn in faces of one colour counts all of them, as PyPI's sidebar cards do, and a code block gets the veil at any height). Lowering the limit would veil buttons, badges and notices too.
- **Boxes of the page's colour holding a picture stay opaque**: a box painting exactly the page's colour goes see-through only when it is empty of pictures and text of its own, so a loaded ad slot (an image or a frame in it, as in Heise's `.us_ad`) or a tile round a photo keeps its colour, and a box smaller than the grid's spacing is found only once one of the grid's points falls on it (or a page layer under it is walked up from).
- **Links show through a slider's arrow made see-through**: a slider's arrow in the colour of its bar or of the page (NZZ's row of sections, Heise's ticker) goes see-through with the fade the site draws beside it, its icon kept, so the links scrolling under it show through the arrow. A round or shadowed arrow over pictures (a carousel's) keeps its fill.
- **A dark consent wall over a light page**: a consent dialog whose dark backdrop covers the whole window when the page loads (watson.ch's, `rgb(24, 25, 27)`) is read as the page, so under a light theme the page is measured as dark while the wall is open, and its header gets the dark veil.
- **Brand sections a dedicated site style clears lose their band under a light theme**: a light-only page whose body paints white is light, but a section in a brand colour that a dedicated site style makes see-through whatever the marks (rust-lang.org's green, purple and red bands, cleared by its bundled style) does not get its colour back: its white text is inked dark on the light glass instead, readable but without the band. A light-only page whose white comes from `<html>` or a wrapper rather than the body still takes its tone from its text. A page that sets its own dark mark on `<html>` again after the add-on took it off under a light theme is followed and stays dark.
- **Dark text on a section the glass cleared**: a heading in a dark brand colour on a light section of a page left see-through under a dark theme (a navy title on corona.com) stays dark on the dark glass when the page is not darkened and the ink pass does not reach it.
- **Bands between the checked rows stay opaque**: page layers are looked for on a 5×5 grid of points across the window, with a row along its top and bottom edges, so a band in the page's colour that falls between two rows of points is not reached (the white band under sbb.ch's timetable search, light theme), and neither is a gradient fading the page's colour there (the white fade at the foot of ch.ch's left menu list, light theme).
- **Proton's sign-up page is always light**: account.proton.me's theme recipe gives its sign-in and account pages their dark theme, but its sign-up page always uses its light storefront theme, whatever the stored value. A first visit landing there marks the recipe failed for that site (Dark Reader darkens it, as before) until the recipes change.
- **Wikipedia's picture frames stay solid**: the infobox, hatnotes and search field go see-through or get a light veil, but the frame around a thumbnail (`figure`, its caption) keeps the wiki's own panel colour, grey at night and off-white by day.
- **Dark logos in shadow trees on a darkened page**: an inline SVG logo drawn with the default black fill in a shadow tree reads dark on dark under a dark theme (the Confederation's wordmark on MeteoSuisse). Not yet handled.
- **A logo in several colours whose wordmark Dark Reader darkened**: Dark Reader reads the fill written on a large inline SVG (or on its `<g>`) as a background colour and darkens it. A white logo of one tone is inverted back to light. tutti.ch's header logo puts a purple wordmark (`fill="#67355c"` on a `<g>`) in the same SVG as a red tile with a white letter, so its wordmark turns a purple close to the dark glass (about 1.4:1), while its footer logo reads light pink. A logo in several colours is not inverted, since that would also invert its tile and letter. Lifting only the darkened group's fill is not yet handled. Likewise, a chart's svg fills Dark Reader darkens (a `#3b82f6` bar turned `rgb(8, 67, 164)`) are left as darkened when the svg also holds `<text>`.
- **An empty light banner stays solid**: a light block holding no text nor element (Reddit's `#subreddit-banner-img`, an empty `rgb(229, 235, 238)` strip over the glass) is reached by no text, so it keeps its fill under a light theme. A mid-grey button with a white label (MDN's "View in English") is not a light fill, and keeps its colours. A lone white card on the glass (Discourse's "Hot topics", YouTube's history notice) keeps its white too, as a box in the page's own colour does: a veil there would also take the white panels pages lay out on purpose. A field veiled out of view loses its veil while the page is darkened, and gets it back once it comes near the window again.
- **Light bands painted by a gradient, and other light-theme leftovers**: a full-width band whose white or near-white comes from a `background-image` gradient rather than its `background-color` (a `linear-gradient(#fff, #fff)` section, an Apple-style `#f5f5f7` to white band, Airbnb's 1400×200 white-to-`#f8f8f8` wrapper behind its header) stays a solid light slab on the glass under a light theme. Also open: MDN's sticky header keeps its dark nav, breadcrumb and banner rows as opaque slabs at the top of the page and only takes the light bar once scrolled; the white line-clamp fade at the end of each MDN home card shows as a white rectangle on the veiled card (its painter is not yet found); Arch Linux's category box (`.catlinks`, 34 px high) is lower than a lone panel needs and stays a light strip on the darker glass.
- **A dark search button in a shadow root keeps its fill under a light theme**: MDN's search pill (`button.mdn-search-button`, `rgb(24, 25, 27)` with a white icon, in the open shadow root of `mdn-search-button`) stays a small black pill in its light header: the add-on finds no box for it (its host reports a 0 by 0 rect), so it is neither veiled nor inked.
- **Closed shadow trees are not read**: the add-on reads the text of open shadow roots (a section kept in its own colour, ink, page layers and blocks), but a closed one is out of its reach, so a dark section drawn there can still lose its colour to the generic style under a light theme.
- **Dark Reader inverts some selected-tab underlines**: on a page Dark Reader darkens, the underline of an active tab drawn in the page's shadow DOM can come out in the inverted colour (MeteoSuisse's day tabs). Not fixed: the line is not a border of the tab button, and it is not a rule or glyph the add-on looks at.
- **Headless snapshots show no blur**: Firefox's page snapshot (drawSnapshot, as the site checks take their screenshots) does not paint `backdrop-filter`, so content under a veiled bar shows sharp in them, behind the 65 % veil only (ARTE's row of chips scrolled under its header, the text under MeteoSuisse's anchor bar). In a real window the bar's blur hides it; a check of the blur itself needs a shown window.
- **A page coloured on its root alone, with no text on its background, keeps the text's tone**: when no text on the page's background decides (a portal whose sections fade in once scrolled to), the add-on reads the colour the body paints, with the generic page rules set aside. The root's own colour is not read (the tone's tint lies on it once measured, so a second read would undo the first), and neither is a body a site style of its own makes transparent: such a page is still judged from the text on its cards and pictures.
- **A bar's labels are judged over the worst of what can scroll under the veil**: a label must read on the dark veil over white too, so a site's muted grey links get the light colour even while dark content passes under them. The light veil is judged over black (see *Text is judged against the glass over a black or a white wallpaper*). A child layer clipped by its `background-clip` is still taken by its whole box; only pseudo-elements are measured by the area they paint.
- **Light sections of a dark page keep their own colour**: a light section whose dark text was drawn for it (GitLab's feature cards and its last call-to-action band) shows in its own white on a dark page rather than as glass, as a dark section of a light page does (MeteoSuisse).
- **Launcher results come after the apps** whose name matches the query, ten lines at most, since the plugin answers in the launcher's *all* mode instead of having a mode of its own.
- **The current site can lag without the companion**: the browser's saved session is about 15 s behind, up to an hour after 3 minutes of system idle.
- **Firefox family only**: there is no Chromium support (see *Not planned*).
- **The niri rule covers the whole app-id.** It matches every window of `firefox`, `librewolf`, `zen` and the rest. A profile without glass, started with `-profile` for instance, is also shown fully opaque instead of following DMS's global opacity.
- **Zen has not been tested live.** Its profile folders and its transparency-mod warning are tested on fixtures only.
- **Flatpak and Snap**: Snap Firefox is not in the niri rule, because its app-id could not be confirmed. Snap Firefox therefore gets the CSS tint and DMS's global opacity together, and looks more transparent than intended. No Flatpak or Snap install has been tried live. A profile outside the known profile folders is not found.
- **Opacity source**: browserGlass reads the opacity of the *first* global window rule (one without `match`) in DMS's `niri/dms/windowrules.kdl`, but niri applies the *last* one. If several global rules set different opacities, the browser can differ from your other windows. Use the opacity override in *Opacity* in that case.
- **A cleaner that waits too long gives up.** When you uninstall while a browser runs, a detached cleaner waits for that browser to close before it puts the prefs back in `prefs.js`. It polls for 7 days at most. After that it leaves its job in the state folder's `pending/` directory, and the next uninstall finishes it. Until then the browser keeps the plugin's pref values.
- **An unreadable profile lock keeps the niri rule.** When a profile's `.parentlock` exists but cannot be probed (permissions or an unusual file system), the profile counts as possibly running. Turning the glass off then keeps the plugin's niri rule, and *Status* shows it as held, until the lock can be read.
- The running-browser check reads the lock with the `struct flock` layout of x86_64 and aarch64 glibc.
- After a plugin update, run `dms restart`: DMS keeps the plugin's JavaScript and component files cached, so a reload alone may still run the old code.
