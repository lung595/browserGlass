# Changelog

All notable changes to Browser Glass are documented here.
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

## [1.4.0] - 2026-11-17

### Added

- **Settings**: a search field on top, a category rail on the left (Status, Opacity, Pages, Sites, Live control, Files & uninstall, Help) and one page at a time; typing shows only the matching settings, editable in place, Esc gives the page back, Ctrl+F or / focuses the field; a narrow window shows the rail as icons.
- **Each site's own dark mode** (with *Live control* and *Dark pages*): many sites with their own theme menu keep the choice in the page's storage or a cookie and show their light theme to a visitor. Under a dark theme the add-on now writes that value before the page's scripts run, so the site starts in its own dark theme instead of being darkened by Dark Reader. A generic rule covers common storage keys (`theme`, `color-mode`, `colorScheme`, `appearance`…) already set to a light value; recipes for given sites live in `data/themes.json` (see `data/themes.md`). The prior value is recorded and put back under a light theme, once the site is excluded, or with *Dark pages* off; a value you change in the site's menu wins and is never written again; a site that stays light after the write gets its value back and Dark Reader as before.
- **Wikipedia** (English, French and German) starts in its own dark theme, signed out: a recipe writes the wiki's theme cookie (`skin-theme-clientpref-night`), keeping the other display preferences stored in that cookie.
- **npm** starts in its own dark theme: a recipe writes its `npm-color-mode` setting.
- **Gemini**, signed out, starts in its own dark theme when its Theme menu was set to light: a recipe writes its `Bard-Color-Theme` setting.
- **docs.rs** and **AlternativeTo** start in their own dark theme: recipes write their `rustdoc-theme` and `theme` settings, light when absent on both sites.
- **RTS** starts in its own dark theme: a recipe writes its `rts/feature/darkmode` setting (a recipe's `localStorage` key may now hold `/`).
- **Galaxus** and **Digitec** start in their own dark theme: a recipe writes their `.themeshade` cookie, which the server renders from, and the page is reloaded once. A light theme already picked in their menu is kept in a cookie scripts cannot read and replace: the page is then reloaded once, stays light, and is darkened by Dark Reader from then on.
- **Excalidraw** starts in its own dark theme: a recipe writes its `excalidraw-theme` setting, light when absent whatever the system scheme.
- **Service-Public** and **info.gouv.fr**, built with the French state's design system (DSFR), start in its own dark theme: a recipe writes its `scheme` setting, which the site itself stores as `light` on a first visit. `system`, already dark under a dark system, is left alone.
- **Home Assistant's demo** (demo.home-assistant.io) starts in its own dark theme: a recipe writes its `demo_theme` setting, which holds JSON and is light when absent even on a dark system, as `{"theme":"default"}` (Auto), which follows the system. Its page is drawn in shadow DOM, which neither the page stylesheet nor Dark Reader reaches, so it was left white. A recipe's `localStorage` value may now hold `;` and `,` (a cookie value still may not).
- **Black logos on a white tile** (with *Live control* and *Dark pages*): on a page Dark Reader darkens, a black logo or line drawing on a transparent background (sponsor logos, a signature) read as black on black. Such a picture is now shown on a white tile, as product photos are. Its pixels can be read only when it comes from the page's own site or is embedded in the page: a picture from another site stays as it is.
- **Proton**'s account pages (account.proton.me: sign-in, and Mail, Calendar and Drive signed out) start in their own dark theme: a recipe writes its `theme` setting, a number (`2` light, `1` dark), absent and light for a new visitor even on a dark system.
- **impots.gouv.fr** (DSFR) gets a recipe for its `scheme` setting: the site stores the scheme it resolved itself, so after a visit under a dark theme it stayed dark under a light one; the value it had is now put back.
- **Python's documentation** (docs.python.org) starts in its own dark theme when its Theme menu was set to light: a recipe writes its `currentTheme` setting. `auto`, which it stores when absent and which follows the system, is left alone.

### Fixed

- Rows inside a glassed header kept their own opaque fill (with *Live control*, a site's own dark theme): Heise's row of categories, in the colour of the header band made glass, stayed a grey slab because it holds an icon, and NZZ's sticky row of sections stayed black under its masthead until it stuck, its slider arrow an opaque black square on the bar's veil once stuck. Now the rows of a low band across the window in its colour go see-through with it, a sticky row across the window in a plain colour of the page's side is glass while still in the flow and gets the veil once stuck, and a button as tall as a bar in its colour (a slider's arrow) goes see-through, its icon and border kept, with the fade it draws over the links into the bar's colour (NZZ's black `::before`, a dark smudge on the glass before). A flat icon button in the page's colour straight on the glass outside a bar (the arrow of Heise's news ticker, a grey or white square) goes see-through too.
- Wide modules and slightly tinted panels stayed full opaque panels instead of the light veil (with *Live control*): Heise's promotion and *Meistgelesen* modules in its own theme (`rgb(50, 50, 50)` on its dark grey page, white on its light one), alone in view, crates.io's panels and crate list (`rgb(20, 20, 19)`, white in its light theme), PyPI's pale blue sidebar cards (`rgb(12, 30, 39)` once darkened) and, in its light theme, its grey sidebar cards and the grey tab band under the project header. Only a row of link cards in the page's plain colour got the veil: now a module across most of the window and lower than it gets it alone, a panel round text that is no link counts as a card, cards from 64 px high count (crates.io's rows), and a slight tint is allowed on both a page's own cards and darkened blocks (a colour written as `color(srgb …)` read too). A band off the light glass by a little less contrast is now a band of the page and goes see-through. A row of dashboard panels in the page's colour (Grafana's) now gets the veil as well, while a card laid over a strong band keeps its fill, as on a darkened page (a row of teaser text boxes pulled up over their photos, Heise's hero teasers, still gets the veil), and a picture set in a tile counts as a product mat only when centred in it (Heise's promotion photo, set to one side, no longer keeps its module solid).
- Rows and boxes painting the page's own colour stayed opaque on the glass (with *Live control*, either theme): watson.ch's feed, a column of rows in the page's black (white in its light theme) stacked edge to edge, each row too short to count as a column, and heise.de's empty ad slots in exactly the page's grey, narrower than the window. A run of rows of one colour, as wide and each starting where the one above ends, is now judged as one column, every row of it glass; a box of exactly one of the page's colours, with no border, shadow or rounded corner, no text of its own and no picture, in the flow and over nothing but its own ancestors, is glass too (`data-browserglass-canvas="fill"`). A border drawn in the colour of a layer made see-through, on it or on a see-through element round it (watson.ch frames its rows and their column with borders in the page's colour), goes see-through with it (`data-browserglass-seam`), so no opaque lines are left between the rows. Text laid over a picture outside the window (the bylines on watson.ch's photo teasers) no longer counts as the page's own text when the add-on reads the page's tone, so its light theme no longer flips to the dark glass once scrolled. A box painting the colour of the page layer it lies on (that layer's colour as it paints it now, so a page switching its theme is read right) counts as the page, not as a card, when the tone is read: once its white rows were glass, the white teasers in them held all the headlines and only the pink section titles were left on the page, so watson.ch's light theme turned to the dark glass right at load. Cards and bands of another colour keep theirs.
- A cookie banner fixed along the bottom sometimes turned see-through, its text inked dark over the headlines under it (with *Live control*, light theme: BBC's dark *Let us know you agree to cookies* banner). The banner comes late, and when its text was read before its box became a bar, its dark panel was kept for that light text, then taken for the bar's own colour and cleared under the veil. A surface kept for its text now stays the site's: it is never cleared as a bar's or a fixed element's fill, so the banner stays opaque whichever comes first.
- Pale grey text tinted blue straight on the light glass stayed hard to read (with *Live control*: the slate grey breadcrumb on Docker's docs, about 2.3:1 over a mid-tone wallpaper). The 3:1 floor for grey text on the light glass took a slight blue tint for a colour and skipped it; a slate grey now counts as grey and gets the readable colour, while a coloured link keeps its own.
- A card pulled up over a band of a strong colour turned into a muddy tinted box (with *Live control* and *Dark pages*: SBB's white journey form, half over the red band of its hero, went dark red, its grey placeholders hard to read). Dark Reader's darkened card got the light veil, see-through to the red behind it. A card whose first element painted behind it is an opaque, saturated colour now keeps Dark Reader's opaque fill, as a card over a picture does.
- A white band across a light page stayed opaque on the glass (with *Live control*: sbb.ch's band under its red hero, holding *Situation de la circulation*, drawn inside a web component). The add-on samples the window at a few rows to find the page's layers, and the band lay between two of them; under a dark theme Dark Reader's darkened blocks were found from the text instead. On a page Dark Reader leaves alone, the text near the window is now followed up too, open shadow roots included: the outermost element painting anything under it, when it is a layer of the page (a plain colour on the page's side, as wide as the window or down a page column, not floating), becomes glass; a card or a header on a page layer keeps its colour, and so does a band along the page's top or bottom edge or holding the site's menus (GitHub's dark masthead, in its own dark theme, is the site's bar, not a band of the page), as long as it is no lighter than the dark glass (darker than the light glass on a light page): IKEA's grey utility bar in its dark theme is glass, not an opaque grey slab over it.
- A sticky row tucked under a higher sticky bar showed through it (with *Live control*: BBC's row of sections, slid up under its compact header once scrolled, printed its links over the BBC logo with a second veil band). The site hides such a row behind the header's opaque fill, which the veil made see-through. A bar under a higher bar that spans its width and covers its top or bottom edge is now clipped to its part outside that bar (`data-browserglass-tuck`), what hangs out of it (a menu) kept; rows stacked side by side (SRF, MDN) stay whole.
- Strips and fades a page draws in its own colour with a `::before` or `::after` stayed opaque on the glass (with *Live control*, either theme, the site's own theme or Dark Reader's): Tages-Anzeiger's lead story kept a solid frame of 40 px gutters down its sides, and the fade at the end of its top menu, into the page's colour under its scroll button, left a smudge over *Das Magazin*. Only the empty pseudo-elements of fixed or sticky elements, and fades over text, were looked at. A pseudo-element of the text's elements painting a plain colour near the page's straight on the glass (no background of the site's own under it) is now see-through (`data-browserglass-filled`), and the pseudo-elements in a bar fading into the page's colour are see-through where they painted it, text or not; one on a card or a band of another colour keeps its colour.
- The strip a story draws over its top in the page's colour stayed opaque on the glass (with *Live control*, either theme): Tages-Anzeiger's `whitecover`, an empty 40 px band across the top of its lead story and of the next, kept a solid bar of the page's background. An empty element beside the text's way up painting an opaque colour near the page's straight on the glass, over nothing but its own ancestors (not a cover hiding the site's content), is now see-through (`data-browserglass-canvas`); one on a card keeps its colour.
- **Buttons lost in their panel** (with *Live control* and *Dark pages*): a button Dark Reader darkens to the tone of what is behind it read as plain text, with no edge (Zalando's white *Alles akzeptieren* in its black consent banner, its black *Change language* in a white popover, both near-black on near-black). A small control whose label reads on its fill but whose fill stands under 1.25:1 from what is behind it, with no border or shadow of its own that shows there, now gets the thin light ring of a dark brand button (`edge` in `data-browserglass-brand`), also in open shadow roots and when drawn late; a light theme takes it off.
- A floating label laid over its field (Swiss Post's tracking number) was read against the form around the field, and given dark ink on the darkened field. A label positioned over a text field, a text area or a list beside it is now read against that field.
- **YouTube's ambient mode** (with *Live control*): on a watch page an opaque near-black panel (`#0f0f0f`), first see-through then solid as it faded in, covered the header, the player and the video's details down to the comments. It is the glow YouTube draws around the player in a small canvas stretched far beyond it, which the add-on's checks of the page's layers never met, as it takes no pointer events. A canvas that takes no pointer events, covers at least half of the window and is stretched at least four times its size (a wash of colour, not a picture) is now hidden on a see-through page; charts, players and games are left alone.
- **Pages built of web components** (with *Live control*: sbb.ch, MeteoSuisse, Home Assistant): the parts a page draws in open shadow roots got no glass, no bar veil and no readable-text check, since the add-on's hit tests stopped at the component's host. They now look inside the open shadow trees they meet: the page's layer in a component becomes glass (also when an empty `::before` paints it, as Home Assistant's view and MeteoSuisse's header band), a fixed header or bottom bar there gets the veil in the page's tone (MeteoSuisse's anchor bar), its parts in the page's colour become glass, icons and glyphs there are handled as on the page, and text over a card of its own is no longer given the glass's ink (SBB's red button keeps its white label).
- A dark section of a light page lost its colour to the glass and left its white text unreadable (with *Live control*: MeteoSuisse's forecast, drawn in the page's shadow DOM, where the add-on did not look). The generic style cleared it like any other `section`. Before light text without contrast is recoloured, the add-on now looks at the surface it sits on as the site paints it: when the text was drawn for that colour (opaque, and every colour of the text reads on it), the surface gets `data-browserglass-own`, which the generic style skips, and keeps its own colour; a dark header row (Le Monde's menu row) and a black button (GitHub's "Sign up") are kept the same way. The add-on also reads the text of open shadow trees now, so on a page Dark Reader darkens such a section is no longer an opaque slab: a wide one is glass, as other page layers are, and a smaller one gets the light veil of a block.
- **Product photos on a dark page** (with *Live control*): a photo whose white background the site wipes out with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` (LDLC's product photos) was inverted since 1.3.3, and showed as a colour negative. Such a picture, a logo blended that way included, is now shown on a white tile with round corners and a thin white margin (the picture's own corners square, so no grey dot shows where they meet the margin), as shops do in their own dark theme, not inverted and not blended. The mark is now `data-browserglass-tile` (it was `data-browserglass-blend`). Icons under 24 px and photos nearly as wide as the window are left as they are.
- A mascot drawn with a black outline round a large coloured face (GitHub's Mona on its signed-out home page, with *Live control* and *Dark pages*) was inverted like a dark logo, and showed a maroon face with green eyes. A picture with two flat tones is now inverted only when its readable part is no larger than its dark ink, an accent on a dark logo; a picture mostly in readable colours is left as it is.
- A product photo loaded lazily (LDLC's new products, scrolled into view) was sometimes left without its white tile, black on black: it had no width until loaded, and when it loaded after the last check nothing looked at it again. A picture is now looked at again once it loads.
- A tall see-through header whose top row is a notice of its own (a country redirect over LDLC's header) never got its veil once scrolled: only the middle line of a see-through bar was checked for content under it, and that line fell inside the notice, so the page's labels showed between the header's links. Content under any part of the bar now counts.
- A thin see-through strip fixed along an edge (GitHub's find-in-page marks down the scrollbar, 15 px wide) got a bar's veil since content under any part of a bar counts, and showed as a pale stripe down the right edge of the window. A strip thinner than 24 px is no bar.
- A site logo drawn as an inline SVG (next.ink's) showed dark on the dark header of a page Dark Reader darkens: Dark Reader judges such a logo by the colours written in its markup, dark, and inverts it, while the page's style sheet paints its shapes, a colour Dark Reader had already lightened, so the invert turned it dark again. Such a logo, its shapes now painted light on a dark background, is shown as painted, without the invert (`data-browserglass-uninvert`); one dark by its markup alone stays inverted.
- A web app dark by its own style (Grafana) got the light glass under a light theme, and Dark Reader on top of its own dark theme under a dark one: its dashboard sits on a panel of its own beside the sidebar, so the only text left on the page was its black "Skip to main content" link, hidden off the page until focused. Text out of sight (a skip link placed off the page, a label for screen readers clipped or in a box of a pixel) no longer counts, and a lone short line on the page gives way to the rest of the text. A `dark`, `dark-mode` or `theme-dark` class or `data-theme="dark"` on `<body>` (Grafana's `theme-dark`) now counts as a page with its own dark version, like the same mark on `<html>`.
- A white page with dark video cards (RTS) was measured as a dark page, and under a light theme got the dark tint and white text: the white titles on the cards outnumbered the page's own dark text. Text on a card that paints a background of its own tells the card's tone, not the page's: the text on the page's background now decides first, the cards' text only when there is nothing else. Text drawn over a picture (a shop's white headlines on its banners) and a dark band as wide as the page that starts below the first screen (a shop's tall footer) count as cards too, so Amazon stays a light page and is darkened.
- Dark pages: a light page whose cookie banner or dialog covers the screen when it loads no longer has that banner taken for the page's background: the banner was made see-through instead of the page.
- Text on buttons whose light face is drawn by a pseudo-element or by a positioned layer inside the button (a light "Upgrade" button on a web app, for example) is no longer recoloured to white over that light face. The pass that keeps text readable now sees those faces.
- Stack Overflow, signed out: the filled buttons ("Ask Question", "Sign up") and the search field lost their fill under Dark Reader and read as bare labels. They get their fill and border back.
- A light page with a dark header and a long menu (Le Monde) was measured as a dark page: the menu was read first, and filled the reading limit before the content, so the light page was left white under a dark theme. The site's menus (its navigation, page header and footer) are now skipped, and count only when the page has no other text.
- A light page with a lot of mid-grey text (Hacker News' grey lines under each title) was measured as a dark page, and under a light theme got the dark tint behind its black titles. Grey text between the two tones now counts only when the page has no other text.
- Dark pages: grey text (Hacker News' lines under each title) turned bright blue under Dark Reader, which gives grey the tint of the theme's text colour; that tint is now muted, so grey text stays grey.
- Dark pages: a frame from another site (a consent dialog, a sign-in prompt) showed a white box, or covered the page with an opaque grey veil (The Guardian's and the BBC's consent dialogs), because Dark Reader gives every frame a dark colour scheme. Frames keep their own colour scheme on a darkened page.
- A button whose label sits over a hover layer kept invisible (opacity 0) took that layer for its face, and its label kept a colour unreadable on the glass (Gemini's "New chat"). An invisible layer no longer counts as a face.
- A link underlined with a thin gradient (2px high, as on mozilla.org's buttons) was taken for text over a picture and never recoloured, white on the light glass. A thin gradient line no longer counts as a picture.
- Text whose colour has a transition (WordPress.com's menus) flickered between its own colour and the readable one: each check read the colour halfway through the transition. The colour is now read without it.
- Light words drawn with a gradient clipped to the text (a headline's highlighted words) were left light on the light glass, unreadable: their own colour is transparent, so they were never checked. Their gradient's colours are now checked, and when none reads, the words get the readable colour drawn over the gradient.
- Text straight on a page's translucent tint is judged against the colour the browser actually shows there. Black text on the dark tint used to be judged readable and was left black.
- Editing the DMS window opacity while a browser still waited for a restart showed no toast: the restart toast came once per restart, so the new opacity looked ignored. It now shows again for each new opacity, and says to close every window of the browser and open it again: opening it from the launcher while one of its windows is still open only adds a window to the browser already running, which keeps its old styles.
- Page surfaces narrower than the window stayed solid panels on the glass: Mastodon's feed column in the middle of the window and the dark panel under its menu, Grafana's dashboard surface right of its menu, Gitea's second menu row across the window. A layer that paints a plain colour on the page's side (dark on a dark page, light on a light one) down a column as tall as most of the window to its bottom, or across the window, now becomes glass like a full-window background; a coloured band, a column filled by a picture or a player, a fixed panel and the cards and panels laid on it keep their colours. A dark app under a light theme (Grafana), its root carrying the dark tint, gets the same. A header as wide as the column it stays in (Mastodon's sticky "Explore" row, painted by the box inside it) now gets a bar's veil, and the strip in the page's colour its `::after` draws under the row's rounded corners becomes glass too (`data-browserglass-filled`); a child faded out to opacity 0 (the scrim of YouTube's menu drawer) does not count as a bar's background.
- A sticky header painted by an empty layer behind its content (Bitwarden's, a positioned child at a negative z-index) got no veil once scrolled, its links straight on the hero picture: the layer, in the page's colour, was made see-through while the header was still below the notice at the top, and the header then read as having no background of its own. A child that covers the bar counts whichever child it is and even once made see-through, so the header gets its veil.
- A drawing board (Excalidraw) was measured as a dark page: its white is painted inside a `<canvas>` filling the window, which no style shows, and the text over it is mostly its welcome screen's pale grey hints. Under a light theme it got the dark tint (hidden behind the board), under a dark one it was never darkened. When no text on the page's background decides, a canvas covering most of the window and painted opaque now gives the tone from its own pixels.
- The menu button of a page Dark Reader darkens (Boursorama's three bars) vanished on the dark glass: its bars are drawn with a background in the site's dark text colour, which Dark Reader takes for a background and darkens. A small glyph drawn that way, a few pixels thick in an empty element inside a button or a link (or in a box as small as an icon), that no longer reads against what is behind it, is now painted in the text colour (`data-browserglass-glyph`, naming the element, its `::before` and its `::after`). Thin rules of the page, separators in a line of text and coloured pills keep their colours.
- White cards and strips of a page Dark Reader darkens showed as opaque near-black panels on the glass (admin.ch's news cards, PostFinance's top strip, Boursorama's blocks). A block in a plain dark colour close to the page's own, holding text and not a picture, a player, a dialog or a floating menu, now gets a light veil of the DMS dark surface (35 %, lighter than a bar's) that keeps its border (`data-browserglass-block`), and the blocks inside it go see-through; a strip or band as wide as the window becomes glass like the page. Buttons, pills and brand-coloured blocks keep their colours, and so do pages in their own dark theme. The text near the window is found however much hidden text comes before it: the blank nodes between tags, a closed menu of a thousand links (Boursorama's header) and text far from the window no longer use up what is read, which left the cards of a right-hand column late in the page (Boursorama's "Mes listes") solid and their text unchecked.
- A black word mark beside a coloured emblem (admin.ch's logo: its text a black picture on a transparent background, its flag another one) showed cut in two on a page Dark Reader darkens: the text got a white tile, the flag stayed outside it. A see-through picture whose dark ink is flat grey or black is now inverted to light ink (`data-browserglass-tile="invert"`) with no tile, and the logo reads whole; coloured or shaded ink (a black product cut out of its photo) keeps the white tile, and product photos are unchanged.
- Thin separators in a light grey (admin.ch's line between its logo and its title) vanished on a page Dark Reader darkens, turned a dark grey on the dark glass. An empty element at most 2 px thick and 8 px long or more, in a plain neutral colour that no longer reads against a dark background, now gets `data-browserglass-rule` and a light neutral rule colour (white at 25 %).
- Buttons in a saturated brand colour went dull on a page Dark Reader darkens: PostFinance's yellow buttons turned olive with a light grey label (2.8:1), Proton's violet ones a dark navy. A small button or link with a short label whose fill (its own, or that of a `::before` or `::after` covering it) has a strong colour that its label reads on now keeps both colours as the page had them (`data-browserglass-brand`); a site style of `data/sites/` for such a button still wins. Buttons drawn after the page was darkened (a cookie banner shown late) still get Dark Reader's colours.
- A sticky header in rows of its own colour kept an opaque band over its veil (MDN's row of links, ARTE's list of links across the middle of its header): only a child covering 90 % of the bar in the page's colour turned to glass, and none at all on a page with no layer of its background. A child of a marked bar painting the bar's own colour (read before the veil, from the bar or the layer backing it) or the page's (close to it, MDN's two dark greys), over 30 % of the bar or as tall as it (ARTE's language menu) and inside it, is now see-through too, and so are the shown parts of such a row in that colour (each of ARTE's links; a menu hidden in the row keeps its colour once opened). The layer behind a header's content that holds its skip links (ARTE's, under a header the generic style clears) is hidden instead (`data-browserglass-under`) until the focus enters it, so the links' text (a stray letter beside the account link) no longer shows through. A sticky header whose top row was glass already (MDN's, below a notice) got no veil once stuck: that row was taken for the page's background under the bar.
- Icons and logos stayed dark on the dark glass. On a page dark by its own style, not darkened by Dark Reader, nothing looked at them: 1Password's black word mark and dark menu chevrons in its veiled header, ch.ch's navy search icons and pictograms. An inline SVG icon drawn in its text colour (`currentColor`) is now checked as text is and takes the readable colour of the labels beside it; a picture with dark ink of one tone on the glass or in a veiled bar is inverted to light ink on such a page too, and a picture from another site is read when its server allows any site to (CORS). Same-style icons were treated unevenly (UBS's 24 px service icons, thin black lines and a red accent: half got a white tile, half nothing): an icon (32 px at most) now never gets a tile, it is inverted when its dark ink is of one tone, its pixels read large enough for thin lines to count. Inverted ink keeps its hues (a red accent stays red, a navy pictogram turns a light blue instead of getting a white tile).
- A light page made of a white column beside a see-through half (ch.ch) was sometimes judged dark, and kept its white column with its text recoloured light under a dark theme. Text the add-on had already recoloured for the dark glass, while the page was still loading, voted with that light colour. Such text now votes with the colour the page gave it, and a white column at least 40 % of the window wide, as tall as most of it and down to its bottom, counts as the page's background when the text is read.
- Text in a see-through fixed or sticky footer or header was judged against the glass even where the page paints white under it (ch.ch's footer over its white column), and got light, unreadable on white. It is now judged against what is painted under it.
- A small label in the colour of a page column made glass (ch.ch's tagline on white) left a white rectangle on the glass under a light theme. It goes see-through with the column; a box laid over the page, such as a popup menu, keeps its colour.
- A page built of sections across the window in a colour near its own (1Password's bands in a slightly lighter grey than its page) kept them opaque, below the first screen as well: only the lowest layer of the page's background turned to glass. A section in the flow at least 90 % as wide as the window, or edge to edge across a narrower page column, painting a colour near the page's over that layer is now one more such layer, judged again as the page scrolls; a section in another colour (a white island, a blue band) and a panel inset on an app's surface keep theirs.

## [1.3.0] - 2026-11-15

### Added

### Security

- **Add-on signature checks** come back at the next browser start even when *Live control* is turned off while the browser runs: until the original prefs are restored in `prefs.js`, the `user.js` block holds their original values (or the browser defaults).
- **Dark Reader's stylesheet fetch** sends no cookies or referrer and accepts only stylesheets, and a page can no longer switch the darkening on under a light system theme by setting its colour variables itself.
- **Uninstall** ignores a file mode out of range in the backup records (it used to apply it as is, or stop on a huge value).
- **A `chrome/` folder that is a dangling symlink** is left alone with a warning; sync used to create its target outside the profile.

### Changed

- **Thicker tone glass**: the glass of a light page under a dark theme (and of a dark page under a light theme) is the DMS surface at 88 % instead of 72 %, so dark text stays crisp over a dark wallpaper instead of sitting on a grey veil.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-11-15

### Changed

- **Live light/dark switch**: the browser tint holds both DMS surfaces and follows the browser's light or dark theme, and *Follow DMS* now writes `content-override = 2` (automatic), so websites follow too. Switching DMS between light and dark no longer needs a browser restart, as long as DMS's *Sync with portal* is on (the settings page says so when it is off).
- **Preferences**: with *Live control* on, the `user.js` block also holds `xpinstall.signatures.required = false` and the profile bit of `extensions.autoDisableScopes` and `extensions.startupScanScopes`, derived from each profile's own values. Their originals are recorded and restored like the other preferences, and the block and its hash are now computed per profile.
- **Gated page CSS**: with *Live control* on, every `http(s)` rule of `dms-glass-content.css` applies only while the page's `<html>` does not carry `data-browserglass-off` (except inside shadow trees, which cannot see it), so the companion can switch it off on an excluded page, and a new section brings the glass back to a site excluded at sync time on pages carrying `data-browserglass-live`, once it is put back. Without the add-on neither mark is set and pages look as before. The gating makes the page stylesheet about 1.9 MB on the bundled styles, against 1.6 MB with *Live control* off, and Firefox 160 parses it in about 23 ms instead of 18. With *Live control* off, none of this gating is written (the page stylesheet differs from v1.0 only by the colour-scheme change under *Fixed*).
- **No restart for a live exclusion**: when the only change is the list of excluded sites and a running browser's companion confirmed it applied the new list, `status` no longer asks that browser for a restart.

### Added

- **Launcher commands**: type `glass` or `gs` in the DMS launcher. `on` and `off` switch the glass, `blist` (alias `fix`) excludes the site open in the browser in front, its whole registrable domain (`account.example.org` -> `example.org`), `unblist` puts it back, and `list` shows the excluded sites (Enter removes one; past ten, the first nine and how many more, narrowed with `gs list <filter>`). The plugin's own view in the launcher (its *Browse* entry) takes the bare words too, and lists the commands when empty. A short toast confirms each action, and says *applies after a restart* when it could not apply at once. New root file `BrowserGlassLauncher.qml`, with its logic in `components/launcher.js`; `plugin.json` gains the `launcher` component and capability.
- **Live control** (*Advanced & help*, on by default): exclusions from the launcher apply to the open pages at once, without a browser restart, through a small companion add-on (`extension/`, `browserglass@companion`) placed in each profile and a native messaging host (`browserglass.py native-host`) that the browser starts. Not available on Firefox release, ESR and Beta builds (they refuse unsigned add-ons), nor on Flatpak and Snap browsers: there, changes apply at the next restart. The *Status* section shows its state, *Files written* lists its files; uninstalling removes the add-on, its preferences, the native manifest and the helper's files.
- **Light pages under a dark theme, with Live control**: the companion measures the page's text colour (in the page, nothing is sent) and marks `<html>` with `data-browserglass-tone`, and the glass follows the page, light glass (the DMS light surface at 72 %) on a light page under a dark theme and dark glass on a dark page under a light theme, for the generic and the dedicated styles alike. It measures again on load, when the page switches its theme on `<html>` and when the colour scheme changes.
- **`active-site --json`**: a new backend command that tells which site a browser window shows, from the companion or else from the browser's saved session (`recovery.jsonlz4`, read with a built-in LZ4 decoder). Its output is in the CLI contract.
- **IPC**: `dms ipc call browserGlass excludeCurrent`, `includeCurrent` and `glass "<command>"` (`on`, `off`, `blist`, `fix`, `unblist`, `list`), for keybindings, on the same path as the launcher and with the same toasts.
- **Status**: a `companion` entry in `status --json` (`enabled`, `state`, `hosts`, `manifest`, and a state per profile), the *Live control* line in the *Status* section and a `companion:` line in the anonymous report; `sync` reports how many browsers received and confirmed a live change.
- **Continuous integration**: the *Tests* workflow (`.github/workflows/tests.yml`) runs the Python tests, the bundled data check and the translation check on every pull request and push to `main`.
- **Site fixes** in `data/sites/`: one CSS file per website, added at the end of its my-internet style (or a new style), with `--browserglass-bar` (the DMS surface at 80 %) for the parts that float over the page. CONTRIBUTING.md explains how to send one.
- **Public Suffix List** in `data/public_suffix_list.dat` (Mozilla, MPL-2.0, see `data/PSL-LICENSE`): the bundled copy that reduces a page host to the site it belongs to (`account.example.org` -> `example.org`), with no network. `tools/update_styles.py` refreshes it, dropping the lines that hold a word of `tools/style-denylist.txt`, and `--check` verifies the bundled copy.
- **Pull request template check**: a pull request keeps the template's sections and shows at least one image of the render; the *PR template* workflow checks it (`tools/check_pr_body.py`), and CONTRIBUTING.md has a "Pull requests" section.

### Fixed

- **Companion removed in about:addons**: an add-on you removed in the browser is no longer taken for a signing refusal (`signing_required`) and placed again, enabled without a prompt, after the next browser update. The profile turns `removed_by_user` (the *Live control* line says the add-on was removed), its preferences are taken back, and nothing is placed until Live control is turned off and on again. LibreWolf, Firefox Nightly and Developer Edition never delete the add-on themselves, so a removal there is always the user's. On other browsers it is told apart once Browser Glass has seen the add-on loaded: the daemon syncs once when a status finds a pending add-on loaded. Turning Live control off or on now syncs at once, so off then on quickly still clears the mark.
- **Excluded sites out of the process list**: the daemon passes the sync settings in a file of `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` (`sync --settings-file FILE`, read then deleted) instead of the `--settings` argument, which every local user could read in `/proc/PID/cmdline`. `--settings JSON` still works.
- **Unreadable text**: white toolbar text on the light tint (the tint now follows the browser theme the text colours come from), and dark text on the dark tint on light-only websites: under a dark theme the generic page rule, the generic site style and now every dedicated site style (my-internet's and `data/sites/`) only apply to pages that declare a dark version, so a light-only page with a dedicated style keeps its own, readable background. Each site style is now written once per colour scheme: the page stylesheet grows from about 0.6 MB to 1.6 MB, and takes about 12 ms more to parse when the browser starts.
- **YouTube**: the header, the category chips and the overlay side menu get the glass tint at 80 % with a blur, instead of putting their text straight over the videos.
- **Proton Mail**: messages show on a light card with a light colour scheme, so text the email leaves unstyled is no longer white on the email's own white boxes.
- **Proton Calendar**: the sidebar, top bar and grid let the glass through instead of painting the theme's solid black; the sticky day headings get the glass tint.
- **Uninstall while a browser runs**: the background `prefs.js` clean-up now survives the logout or shutdown that closes the browser (it keeps checking for a few seconds), the add-on's scope preferences get a value back instead of being deleted after a lost `state.json`, and the folders made for the state folder are removed even after a lost `state.json` or a second uninstall.
- **Disabling during a sync**: a sync the plugin started just before it was disabled could take the state lock after the uninstall and write the glass, `user.js`, the companion add-on and the native manifest back, leaving the browsers glassy with the plugin off. `sync --if-plugin` now also refuses, writing nothing, when DMS's `plugin_settings.json` has the plugin disabled, checked under the state lock.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-11-14

First public release: a rewrite of the earlier, unpublished v0 scripts (a bash `browser-glass-sync` that needed jq and awk, a Python `browser-glass-sites` that downloaded the site styles, and a daemon-only plugin). Settings, excluded sites and the files v0 wrote are taken over on the first sync.

### Added

- **One backend, `browserglass.py`**: a single Python file, standard library only (Python 3.9 or later), run as `python3 -I`. Its commands are `sync`, `status --json`, `uninstall`, `report` and `sites --json`, plus the internal `clean-prefs`. `sync`, `status --json`, `uninstall` and `sites --json` each print one JSON line, `report` prints the anonymous plain-text report and the internal `clean-prefs` prints nothing (the contract is frozen in `tests/fixtures/contract/schema.json`). A second identical sync writes nothing.
- **A composite DMS plugin**: a daemon, a DankBar widget and a settings page (`plugin.json` type `composite`).
  - The daemon syncs when the plugin settings, DMS blur, the light or dark mode, DMS's window rules or DMS's colours change. Syncs are debounced and run one at a time, with no timer.
  - The bar widget is an icon only: `blur_on` while the glass is on, `blur_off` when it is off, and a dot while a running browser must restart. Its menu shows the detailed status and has a glass switch (it turns the glass off; it does not uninstall), *Exclude copied URL* and *Open settings*.
  - The settings page has four sections: *Status*, *Appearance*, *Sites* and *Advanced & help*.
  - Toasts for errors, for a restart that is needed (only when a running browser's files changed), for the first run and for the uninstall.
  - `dms ipc call browserGlass sync | status | toggle | excludeClipboard | report` for keybindings and scripts.
- **Opacity in step with DMS**: by default the glass takes the opacity of DMS's first global window rule, and the tint follows DMS's surface colour. An optional override (a switch and a slider) sets the browser's own opacity. The glass needs DMS blur on and an opacity below 1. Otherwise it turns itself off, and the browser follows DMS's global rule again.
- **Site theme**: *Follow DMS* (dark or light), *Always dark* or *Leave as is*. *Leave as is* puts back the browser's own colour-scheme setting.
- **Special pages**: the new tab page, the home page and the private browsing page are glass. Reader view and PDFs stay opaque by default, and private windows are glass by default. Reader view and PDF share one switch, and the new tab page and private windows each have their own. The selectors were checked in a real headless Firefox.
- **Site styles**: about 650 dedicated styles from [sameerasw/my-internet](https://github.com/sameerasw/my-internet) (MIT), bundled in `data/`. Each one can be turned off from a searchable list. A generic style covers the other sites and has its own switch.
- **Per-site exclusions**: a list of domains that keep their own background, and *Exclude copied URL*, which adds the domain of the URL on the clipboard.
- **Status and report**: the *Status* section and the widget menu show whether the glass is on and, if not, why. They also show the opacity and its source, the tint, the niri rule, the browsers and profiles found, a pending restart and the warnings. *Copy report* copies an anonymous text: versions, profile counts per browser and install type, the state and the warnings. The report has no path, no profile name or id and no URL.
- **Browsers**: Firefox, LibreWolf, Zen, Floorp and Waterfox, installed natively, as a Flatpak or as a Snap. Profiles come from `profiles.ini` and from the new profile manager's profile groups. A running browser is detected from the lock on its profile, without touching it.
- **Zen**: a warning when a Zen transparency mod or transparency pref is found. Nothing is changed on Zen's side.
- **Automatic v0 migration**: the v0 `@import` lines and `user.js` block are removed before the one-time backup is taken. The v0 cache (`~/.cache/dms/browserGlass/`) is deleted, and the v0 settings (`pagesTransparent`, `excludedSites`) are kept. The pref values v0 wrote are recognised as the plugin's own. A sync over a v0 install leaves no duplicate.
- **Byte-for-byte uninstall**: disabling the plugin removes everything it wrote. `userChrome.css`, `userContent.css` and `user.js` come back from a backup taken before the first write, with their modes and symlink targets. Each pref gets its original value back instead of being deleted. The niri include file and its line in `config.kdl` go, and so do the state folder and the v0 cache. When a browser is running, a detached cleaner waits for it to close and then cleans `prefs.js`.
- **Sweep**: deleting the plugin folder also uninstalls. The uninstaller runs from the script text held in memory, so no file needs to remain. It waits 4 s and stops if the plugin comes back (an update or a reinstall). Only after that does it remove the plugin's entry from DMS's plugin settings.
- **French translation** (`translations/fr.json`) of every visible string, checked by `tools/check_translations.py`.
- **Tests**: 502 Python tests on a fake home, 7 offscreen QML test files on DMS stand-ins, and an opt-in end-to-end test in a throwaway headless Firefox.
- **Repository tooling**: `tools/update_styles.py` and a weekly GitHub Action that refreshes the bundled styles from my-internet and opens a pull request. There are issue and pull request templates, and `tools/media/` builds the screenshots from made-up data.

### Changed

- **No runtime dependency other than Python 3**: jq, awk and the bash script are gone.
- **No network at runtime**: v0 downloaded the my-internet styles every 6 hours. They are now bundled in `data/` and refreshed by a pull request.
- **No idle work**: v0 ran a 6-hour timer. The daemon now runs nothing until something changes, and it checks the status every 10 s only while a browser must restart.
- **Glass on and off without a double tint**: the niri rule that keeps the browser window opaque stays until no running browser still has the glass CSS loaded. Only then is it removed.
- **Safer file writes**: writes are atomic, keep each file's permissions, follow symlinks to their targets, skip read-only files with a warning and never write a file whose content is unchanged.
- The plugin needs DMS 2.0 or later. It declares the `process` permission, and its code and description are now in English.

### Removed

- `browser-glass-sync` and `browser-glass-sites`, replaced by `browserglass.py`.
